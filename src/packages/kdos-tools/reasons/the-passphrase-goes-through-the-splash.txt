title: the LUKS prompt goes through the splash, and reads from /dev/tty1
cite: initramfs init
port: cryptsetup

console= is ttyS0 on this kernel command line and the LAST one wins, so a
plain `read -p` prompts a serial port nobody is looking at while the screen
shows a splash that appears to have frozen. The keystrokes come from
/dev/tty1, which is where the keyboard is.

The passphrase is fed to cryptsetup --key-file=- on stdin, never as an
argument: /proc/<pid>/cmdline is world-readable for the life of the process.
Three attempts, then a shell rather than a reboot loop.

And root=UUID= names the INNER filesystem while cryptdevice= names the
container. Confusing the two is the whole trap: the filesystem does not exist
until the container is open.
